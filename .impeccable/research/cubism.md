# Cubism (1907–1920s): research dossier, condensed

Sources: Tate "Cubism" art term, MoMA (Picasso: Guitars 1912–1914), Met (Leonard A. Lauder Cubist Collection; Braque Fruit Dish and Glass), Art Institute of Chicago (Gris, Portrait of Picasso), Guggenheim, Britannica and Dezeen (Cassandre), MoMA/Pompidou (Sonia Delaunay).

## Heart → page rules
a) Multiple viewpoints at once ("broken into planes to show several viewpoints in one space", Tate): each content block is built from 3–7 planes, each showing a different "angle" (front, profile, top edge, label). The headline is the one plane always face-on, so it reads.
b) The object analysed into planes (Analytic 1908–12): small overlapping facets shaded on one side only, near-monochrome. Text never sits on a facet edge; each text block gets its own flat plane.
c) Passage: a plane's edge dissolves into the next plane or the ground. Never close a box on all four sides: one or two hard edges (charcoal line, shadow side), the others fade. This is the biggest difference from low-poly, which closes every facet.
d) Flat picture plane: no real perspective, no vanishing points, no 3D camera. Depth from overlap and value only, small 2D rotations; shift planes in 2D rather than rotate in 3D.
e) Fragment and reassemble: recognisable signs (sound hole, strings, pipe, violin scroll, a letter fragment) cue the viewer to rebuild the whole. Display labels may be clipped ("JOU" for journal) if the full word is always in the DOM or appears on hover/focus.
f) Collage, papier collé (Synthetic 1912–14): real materials pasted on: newspaper, wallpaper, oilcloth, sheet music; simpler shapes, brighter colour. Content cards are cut paper, each with its own material, pinned to a flat ground and drawn over in charcoal.

## Works and their grammar
- Picasso: Demoiselles (1907, shattered curtain planes); Portrait of Ambroise Vollard (1910, dense grey-brown facets, face legible from a few anchors: model for a cursor-reactive portrait); Ma Jolie (1911–12, dissolved figure, crisp stenciled "MA JOLIE" + treble clef: text is the most solid thing); Still Life with Chair Caning (1912, printed oilcloth, oval canvas, rope frame, "JOU"); Guitar (1912 card/1914 sheet metal: the sound hole projects forward, inverted volume); Three Musicians (1921, flat interlocking jigsaw shapes, Harlequin diamonds, black ground).
- Braque: Houses at L'Estaque (1908, ochre/green cubes); Violin and Candlestick (1910, dense passage, silvery grey-brown); The Portuguese (1911, stenciled "D BAL" and numerals asserting the flat surface); Fruit Dish and Glass (1912, first papier collé, faux-bois wallpaper beside charcoal, "BAR", "ALE"; he used house-painter's graining combs).
- Juan Gris: Portrait of Picasso (1912, planes organised on a regulated diagonal grid, cool blue/brown/grey: Cubism on a grid, the most useful for web); The Sunblind (1914); Still Life with Checked Tablecloth (1915, checked pattern as a plane, shifted grid bands each a different view).
- Léger: Contrast of Forms (1913–14, "tubism": cylinders, primaries, thick black outlines, white highlights); The City (1919, flat sign-letters, poster colour).
- Robert Delaunay: Eiffel Tower series; Simultaneous Disc (1912–13, concentric segmented colour discs, Chevreul's simultaneous contrast; "Orphism", Apollinaire).
- Sonia Delaunay: simultaneous textiles; La Prose du Transsibérien (1913, "the first simultaneous book", 2m accordion fold, pochoir colour beside the poem): model for a vertical strip.
- Metzinger & Gleizes, Du Cubisme (1912), Section d'Or: readable figures with faceted overlay, proportion.
- Duchamp, Nude Descending a Staircase No. 2 (1912): figure repeated in overlapping planes along a diagonal, motion arcs.
- Czech Cubism (Gočár, House of the Black Madonna 1912; Janák furniture): crystalline faceting as structure for functional objects (buttons, frames, edges).
- Cassandre (Étoile du Nord, Nord Express 1927; Bifur): stencil and airbrush poster geometry, lettering built into the composition.

## Type
Letters are the anchors of reality: flat, frontal, stenciled, often cut off. Registers: stencil caps with bridge gaps (section names, buttons); newspaper serif (prose); Cassandre-style geometric poster caps.
Google Fonts (verified): **Stardos Stencil** (serif stencil, closest to Braque's painted letters: section titles), **Big Shoulders Stencil** (variable condensed stencil caps: big clipped headings, nav), Allerta Stencil / Saira Stencil One (buttons, labels), **Old Standard TT** (19th-century newspaper roman: body), Libre Caslon Text (alt body), Federo / Poiret One (thin Cassandre-like caps, sparingly), Limelight (occasional poster word), Special Elite (dates on scraps).
Rules: body text on a flat plane, horizontal, ≥16px, newspaper serif, never fragmented. Fragmentation only for display words. Wide letter-spacing on stencil caps. Rotation only on display words, within ±6°.

## Colour and material
Analytic (default state): canvas ground #E8DCC4, raw sienna #B8894A, ochre #C9A15F, burnt umber #5C4128, warm grey #8E8577, Gris grey-blue #6E7F8C, charcoal #2A2622, chalk #F3EEE2.
Synthetic/Orphic accents (earned per section or on interaction): Léger red #C8372D, cobalt #2F4FA2, chrome yellow #E5B42A; Delaunay orange #E07B39, violet #6B4C9A, emerald #3E8E6B, sky #7FB3D5; newsprint #EDE6D3 with ink #1F1D1A; wallpaper green #7A8F6A.
Materials (subtle): faux bois (comb-drawn umber grain over ochre), chair-caning lattice, newsprint columns, sheet-music staves, flocked wallpaper repeat, sand-grain noise, charcoal hatching on one side of a plane only.

## Composition
Analytic pyramid/oval: dense facets at centre thinning to the corners (some canvases literally oval). Gris's regulating diagonal grid. Overlapping planes at partial opacity. Passage edges. Viewpoint shifts between bands. Quiet corners.
Layout ideas: intro as an oval portrait (faceted abstract planes, headline on one frontal plane, papier-collé scraps around the oval); work as a Gris grid (each job one band seen from a different angle and material: newsprint, faux bois, checked plane; bands shifted a few px); skills as a still-life table (tilted tabletop seen from above under objects seen from the side, each object a skill group labelled in stencil on sheet-music scraps); projects as a Prose du Transsibérien accordion strip (Delaunay colour fields left, text right, one fold per project); contact as a Cassandre poster (one big geometric form, stencil headline, one call to action).

## Motion (a change of viewpoint, not a flourish: 180–420ms, ease-out, slight settle, no bounce, paper friction)
1. Faceted portrait: 6–9 planes, each a different view; horizontal cursor movement shifts which view is frontmost per plane (translate a few px, swap stacking), no 3D, ~250ms ease-out.
2. Passage hover: card's open edges bleed further into the next plane (mask feather ~24px over 300ms), hard charcoal edge darkens; leave 200ms.
3. Papier-collé entry: cards drop onto the ground one at a time (±3° random rotation, shadow contracts like paper meeting glue, 1–2px settle, ~60ms stagger, 280ms each), then charcoal lines draw across the pasted edges (400ms).
4. Stencil reveal: stencil caps fill bridge-first like spraying (mask wipe per letter, 30ms stagger); clipped words complete on hover/focus.
5. Czech-Cubist facet button: flat plane with one hatched shadow side; on hover the light source moves (hatching jumps to the opposite side, face lightens one step, 150ms hard step); press shifts 2px into its shadow.
6. Faux-bois comb drag: over the work band a comb stroke follows the cursor leaving wavy umber grain that fades in ~1.5s.
7. Duchamp chronophotographic transition: outgoing content leaves as 4–6 stepped overlapping ghost copies along a diagonal (lower opacity, umber tint, ~50ms apart, discrete); incoming assembles in reverse.
8. Delaunay disc loader/scroll indicator: concentric segmented rings counter-rotating slowly (8–20s/turn); on scroll the rings step one segment to mark progress.
9. Ballet Mécanique rhythm for lists: items enter on a hard repeating rhythm (on, on, pause, on), ~120ms metronomic, linear.
10. Analytic → Synthetic on scroll: palette moves from ochre/grey monochrome to Synthetic brights and facets merge into fewer, larger, flatter shapes as you go down. Scrubbed by scroll.
11. Refraction cursor: a small translucent plane follows with ~100ms lag; content under it shifts 2–4px sideways like a glass pane (text stays legible).
12. Pin and unpin (contact): scraps lift a corner on hover (shadow only under the lifted part); click peels and copies (220ms ease-out).
Reduced motion: opacity crossfades only.

## Clichés to avoid
Random low-poly triangle meshes (Cubist planes are irregular quadrilaterals with open edges); literal cubes or isometric boxes; full 3D camera rotations; rainbow palettes everywhere (colour is earned); pastiche Picasso faces with profile eyes, guitars as clip art; fragmenting body text; glassmorphism as translucent planes (Cubist translucency is flat overlap); Art Deco gold-and-black glamour; sepia filters; constant motion everywhere (hold still, move on interaction).
