# Pop Art (1956–1970): research dossier, condensed

Sources: Tate glossary and Tate Etc. 30, Art UK, Art Institute of Chicago ("What's in the dots"), Cameron Art Museum and AGO (Silver Clouds), Smarthistory, Phillips, Missoula Art Museum (Corita), Lichtenstein catalogue raisonné.

## Heart → page rules
Hamilton's definition (letter, 16 Jan 1957): "Popular, Transient, Expendable, Low cost, Mass produced, Young, Witty, Sexy, Gimmicky, Glamorous, Big Business."
- Mass production: every element is a printed unit stamped from one master, each copy with small print variation (offset, hue swap, uneven ink).
- Consumer packaging: content as products. A project is a box with a front-of-pack logotype, "net weight" (stack), ingredients (tech). Skills on a shelf. Contact is the checkout.
- Celebrity image: one flat-printed source image repeated and colour-shifted (never a casual photo; we have no photos, so an invented flat print).
- Repetition/seriality: grids of identical items where variation carries meaning (Warhol's 32 soup cans, Ferus 1962).
- Irony/deadpan: ad copy undercut with wit ("NEW! IMPROVED! NOW WITH TYPESCRIPT"); cool, never winking too hard.
- Commercial image elevated: crops enlarged and framed like museum art; price tags, barcodes, "Net Wt." at gallery scale.
British Pop (Hamilton, Paolozzi) = analytical collage; American Pop (Warhol, Lichtenstein) = flat, hard-edged, impersonal.

## Works and their grammar
- Hamilton, "Just what is it…" (1956): room assembled from ads, scale collisions, one word ("POP") placed as an object.
- Paolozzi, I was a Rich Man's Plaything (1947), Bunk! (ICA 1952): hard scissor edges, layered printed scraps, lecture-slideshow format.
- Warhol: soup cans (same template, different flavour text); Marilyn Diptych 1962 (off-register colour left, black impressions fading right); Brillo Boxes 1964; Flowers 1964; Silver Clouds 1966 (floating metallized pillows); silkscreen: colour blocks laid before the black key, misregistration, ink starvation/overload.
- Lichtenstein: Ben-Day dots through a stencil as tone, flat Magna, thick uniform black contours (AIC); Whaam! (diptych action), Drowning Girl (close-up), Brushstrokes (gesture made deadpan).
- Oldenburg: giant sagging soft versions of everyday objects (Floor Burger 1962).
- Wesselmann: flat colour areas beside glossy ad cut-outs.
- Rosenquist, F-111 (1964–65, ~86 ft, 51 panels): billboard-scale fragments cropped until half-recognisable, horizontal panorama.
- Robert Indiana, LOVE (1964): stacked square word-block, one tilted letter, complementary colour vibration.
- Jasper Johns: flags, targets, stencil numerals on grids, encaustic over newsprint.
- Corita Kent: serigraphs from supermarket logos and slogans, type torn/folded/warped, text as the main element, joy.
- Blake & Haworth, Sgt. Pepper (1967): crowd of life-size cut-outs.

## Type
Pop copied found lettering: packaging logotypes (script, fat slab), supermarket price cards (condensed grotesque, hand-painted numerals), billboard sans, comic lettering, stencils, Corita's warped type.
Google Fonts (verified): **Ultra** (fat Clarendon slab: Indiana stacks, can logotypes, section names), Alfa Slab One (packaging, "NET WT"), **Anton** / League Gothic (billboard and price-card sans for giant cropped headlines), Big Shoulders Stencil (Johns numerals for dates), Archivo Black (Corita treatments, warp done in layout), Archivo Narrow bold italic caps (rare caption box), **Familjen Grotesk** or Archivo (body). Avoid Bangers/Comic Neue. Max three faces per screen. Type is an object printed into flat colour with misregistration.

## Colour (flat silkscreen inks)
Paper #F4EEDC (never pure white), key black #111111, process red #E2231A, process yellow #FFD400, blue #0070C0, cyan #2EB4E6, Warhol pink #FF5FA2 (fluoro #FF2E88), turquoise #1FC3B5, Day-Glo orange #FF6A13, lime #A6D608, silver #C9CCD1→#F2F3F5 (only gradient allowed, for Silver Clouds).
Flat fills; black key layer offset 2–6px from colour in a consistent direction; 2–3 inks per print, rotated per instance in serial grids; Ben-Day dots only as tone inside shapes, never as full-page wallpaper; complementary pairs at equal value for vibration, sparingly. Dark variant: black paper with fluoro inks.

## Composition
Gapless grid of repeats; seriality with one variable per cell; billboard crop; diptych (colour vs fading monochrome); frontal product shot on flat ground; close-up panel with thick black frame and one caption box; collage with scissor edges and scale collisions.
Layout ideas: intro as a Hamilton room (TV screen holding the portrait, a framed poster of the role, a giant object carrying one word; scraps link into sections); skills as a shelf of cans (one logotype template, different flavour per skill, inks rotate per row; hovering one makes it the frontal hero while others dim); work as a Rosenquist billboard panorama (horizontal panels, cropped fragments, stencil date numerals, hard seams); projects as Brillo-style boxes (name on every face, specs on the side; detail as a diptych); contact as the checkout (price tag, receipt).

## Motion (mechanical and stepped like a print shop; exceptions: Silver Clouds drift, Oldenburg softness)
1. Silkscreen pull: a squeegee bar wipes across ~450ms laying one flat ink; a second pull lays the black key 120ms later slightly off-register.
2. Misregistration hover: colour layer slides 3–5px from the key in ~90ms; snaps back on leave; on press the layers align (offset 0).
3. Serial cascade: grids fill cell by cell in reading order, 30–40ms stagger, hard cuts (no fades), a different ink combination per cell.
4. Ink starvation: repeated images thin out in hard steps (grain up, coverage down) toward the end of a series.
5. Screen Test hold: the portrait stays still, occasionally plays a tiny slowed, steppy movement (blink, slight turn).
6. Silver Clouds: 2–3 silver pillows drift upward on slow sine paths (6–10s), nudged by the cursor with damping.
7. Ben-Day scale: dot radius tracks hover/scroll; dots swell as an element comes closer.
8. Comic panel reveal: black border draws, then the flat fill, then the caption box stamps on with a slight rotation.
9. Box rotation: hover/drag turns a product a quarter turn (~300ms, stepped spring) to show its back panel.
10. Price-tag stickers: "NEW!", "50% MORE CODE" slap on as cards enter (scale 1.3→1 in ~140ms, random rotation, squash on impact); a second sticker can overlap the first.
11. Oldenburg squash: big buttons slump 8–12% vertically and widen on press, wobble ~400ms, slowly recover.
12. Broadcast transition between worlds: TV channel-flip with hard horizontal roll bars (<300ms).
Reduced motion: static offsets, hard cuts, clouds stop.

## Clichés to avoid
POW/BAM/WOW speech bubbles, Bangers/Comic Neue; halftone wallpaper across the page; four-colour Warhol filter on a selfie as the hero; gradients, glass, soft shadows, rounded friendly UI; pure white; an unlimited rainbow; copying real artworks, logos or brands (Campbell's, Brillo, Coca-Cola): invent your own packaging; "fun" without the deadpan commercial voice; 1980s Memphis/Haring squiggles.
